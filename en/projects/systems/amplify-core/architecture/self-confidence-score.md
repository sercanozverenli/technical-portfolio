# Self-Confidence Score (SCS)

## What does this layer do?

The previous layers determined whether the data is healthy (DRS) and which regime the data should be routed to (Routing Engine). However, for an AI system to operate safely in the real world, it must answer one final question:

> "The data was processed and the model produced a prediction; but how much can we trust this final prediction, and can we execute this decision autonomously without human intervention?"

The **Self-Confidence Score (SCS)** is an "autonomy and confidence label" between 0.0 and 1.0 attached to every prediction produced by the system. Through this score, the system stands behind its decision: if the score is high, it executes the decision autonomously; if it is moderate, it submits it for human approval; if it is low, it labels the output as "untrusted".

---

## How is SCS calculated? — A balanced two-component score

SCS does not look solely at data quality or solely at model self-confidence. It combines both in a logical equilibrium:

$$SCS = 0.70 \times DRS + 0.30 \times G$$

- **DRS (Data Reliability Score — 70% weight):** The statistical foundation and quality of the data on which the decision is built.
- **G (Confidence Signal — 30% weight):** The internal degree of certainty of the output produced by the prediction model.

**Why 70% Data Quality, 30% Model Confidence?**  
AI models can sometimes appear extremely confident even when making entirely incorrect predictions (false confidence). Therefore, the model's self-reported confidence is capped at 30%, while the primary foundation (70%) is tied to objective data quality. A prediction derived from poor data cannot achieve a high confidence score, regardless of how confident the model claims to be.

---

## Confidence Signal ($G$): How does it work in classification and regression?

The Confidence Signal ($G$) is calculated on a $[0, 1]$ scale across different task types under the rule **"higher value = higher confidence"**:

1. **In Classification Models (UJIIndoorLoc, Yahoo Finance):** The margin between the model's top predicted class and second top predicted class ($Prob_{max1} - Prob_{max2}$) is used. As the margin widens, the model becomes more decisive.
2. **In Regression Models (Olist):** The width of the Bootstrap Confidence Interval calculated for the prediction is used. The inversion is incorporated directly into the signal definition rather than the score formula itself:
   $$G_{raw} = 1 - \text{normalize}(\text{confidence interval ratio})$$
   Thus, as the confidence interval narrows (uncertainty decreases), the Confidence Signal ($G_{raw}$) approaches 1.

---

## Fallback Model and Structural Safety Cap ($G_{cap} = 0.90$)

Data coming directly from the Corrupted regime (raw DRS: 0.25–0.49) or failing to reach the Clean threshold post-stabilization (`Recovered DRS`: 0.25–0.799) is routed to the **Fallback Model**.

The Fallback Model is inherently simpler and more conservative. A **structural cap of $G_{cap} = 0.90$** is applied to any Confidence Signal generated by this model, regardless of how assertive the prediction appears:

$$G = \min(G_{raw}, 0.90)$$

### Mathematical Safety Guarantee

The maximum achievable `Recovered DRS` score entering the Fallback Model is $0.799$. Consequently, the **theoretical maximum SCS score** achievable by a Fallback Model output is:

$$SCS_{max} = (0.70 \times 0.799) + (0.30 \times 0.90) = 0.5593 + 0.2700 = 0.8293$$

$$SCS_{max} = 0.8293 < 0.85$$

**What does this cap mean?**  
Regardless of how data reaches the Fallback Model and no matter how confident the model claims to be, its output **can never mathematically reach the Autonomous Decision threshold ($\ge 0.85$).** The system structurally prevents any prediction derived from degraded data from running autonomously without human verification.

---

## Three Autonomy Levels — Thresholds and Decision Zones

Based on the final calculated SCS value, the system assigns one of three decision labels:

| SCS Range | Autonomy Level | What it Means / Action Taken |
|---|---|---|
| **≥ 0.85** | **Autonomous Decision** | High data quality, confident model. The prediction is executed directly and autonomously without human intervention. |
| **0.60 – 0.849** | **Human Approval Recommended** | Prediction is generated but too risky for full autonomy. The decision is flagged for operator/human verification. |
| **< 0.60** | **Untrusted Output** | Extremely high uncertainty even if a prediction was generated. Output is rejected, operation is locked. |

## Special Case: Invoice Processing Agent Exemption

The **Invoice Processing Agent** module in the Amplify Core architecture operates under a different operational paradigm than other models:

- Unlike classification or regression domains, the Invoice Agent does not contain a separate predictive model beyond the DRS evaluation.
- The DRS decision itself (Clean / Noisy / Abstention) IS the **final system output.**
- Since SCS by definition measures the output confidence of a predictive model, **SCS is not calculated in the Invoice Processing Agent.**
- Any unapplicable indicator masks ($m_i=0$) are stored directly within the DRS metadata record rather than an SCS report.

---

## Why was this design chosen?

- **Why is SCS not calculated before passing the Confidence Gate ($\tau$)?** The Confidence Gate acts as a live post-inference filter. Records failing the gate transition directly to Abstention (Safe Standby), avoiding unnecessary SCS calculation overhead.
- **Why a formula-based cap ($G_{cap}=0.90$) instead of empirical filtering?** AI safety standards require mathematical proofs over heuristic bounds. The inequality $0.8293 < 0.85$ grounds the system's safety guarantee in a mathematical proof rather than empirical trial-and-error.
- **Transparency and Auditability:** Every prediction output logs `DRS`, `Confidence Signal (G)`, `confidence_signal_source`, and `confidence_signal_capped`. This ensures full retroactive auditability as to why a decision was autonomous or flagged for human approval.
